UrbanSim

UrbanSim helps planners decide where cooling should start and which nature-based solutions fit each street, before anything is drawn. UrbanSim proposes nature-based solutions from evidence and transparent rules.

01 / The Problem

Cities need to know where to act and what actually works.

  1. 01
    Rising urban heat

    Extreme urban heat threatens public health and makes everyday walking increasingly difficult, particularly for vulnerable populations such as older adults.

  2. 02
    Where should cities intervene?

    Heat exposure alone does not reveal where cooling interventions are most needed. Cities must consider how environmental exposure intersects with everyday walking routes and essential destinations to identify priority locations.

  3. 03
    Which solutions actually fit?

    Nature-based solutions are not one-size-fits-all. Their suitability depends on local climate, street geometry, available space, and existing infrastructure. General recommendations do not always translate into site-specific design decisions.

In Sweden, the gap is widest for heat: only 10–15% of municipalities show heat risks or measures on a plan map, against more than 60% for cloudburst flooding (Boverket, 2025).

02 / The Solution

UrbanSim connects climate priorities with context-specific nature-based solutions.

  1. 01
    PrioritiseWhere to act?In the MVP

    Combine solar exposure, surface heat, and proximity to everyday destinations to identify streets where cooling interventions should be prioritised.

    The priority score is transparent and adjustable: each factor's weight can be changed in the MVP.

  2. 02
    MatchWhat fits?In the MVP

    Screen nature-based solutions against local street characteristics, including width, enclosure, and street typology, using transparent suitability rules.

  3. 03
    LearnWhat has worked elsewhere?In development

    Connect potential interventions to documented built projects, their performance evidence, and relevant site conditions to support more informed design decisions.

    The evidence-based precedent library is under development. The MVP includes an illustrative sample library that is not yet verified.

The method builds on my MSc thesis in Kadıköy: the social context decides where to act, the physical context decides how. The MVP runs on the thesis area, Caferağa and Moda, using streets from OpenStreetMap, building heights from GlobalBuildingAtlas and surface heat from Landsat.

UrbanSim is a rule-based decision-support prototype. It does not predict cooling performance or run microclimate simulations; it shortlists options so that detailed, physics-based evaluation can focus where it matters.
