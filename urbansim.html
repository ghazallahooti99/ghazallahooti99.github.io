UrbanSim

UrbanSim helps planners decide where cooling should start and which nature-based solutions fit each street, before anything is drawn. UrbanSim proposes nature-based solutions from evidence and transparent rules; physics-based tools evaluate them. No machine learning generates results.

Programme Sustainability Leapfrogs · LU Innovation, Lund University

01 / The Problem

Planners are asked to use nature-based solutions (NBS) to cool cities, but lack a quick way to see where to start and what fits a given street. Simulation tools test a design once it exists, and NBS catalogues list measures without reference to place.

In Sweden, heat is where the gap is widest: only 10–15% of municipalities show heat risks or measures on a plan map, against more than 60% for cloudburst flooding (Boverket, 2025).

02 / How It Works

UrbanSim follows four questions, in the order a planner asks them. It builds on the method of my MSc thesis in Kadıköy: the social context decides where to act, the physical context decides how. The MVP runs on the thesis area, Caferağa and Moda: streets from OpenStreetMap, building heights from GlobalBuildingAtlas and surface heat from Landsat.

Every suggestion comes from written rules with a cited source and an evidence grade, not from a black-box model, and no temperature reduction is shown unless it can be sourced.
