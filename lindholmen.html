Götaverken: Aquascape Future

An urban design for the rising tides at Lindholmen, Gothenburg.

Typology Waterfront regeneration, sea-level rise adaptation
Location Götaverken, Lindholmen, Gothenburg
Tools Used AutoCAD, Rhino, QGIS, Illustrator, Enscape
Timeline 2024

The Site

Götaverken was once an island, then a working quay, and has stood abandoned for decades. Now it faces a new pressure: rising seas.

Instead of walling the site off with barriers, the project lets the water in and uses it to reshape the place.

Why Water

Water has defined Götaverken at every stage, first as an island and then as a quay built for ships. Keeping water at the centre of the design protects that identity and carries the site’s history into its next life.

Protection

Sea-farming and swimming pools. Pools along the edge absorb flooding, can be cleaned of contamination, and host marine farming and swimming.
Floating, expandable edges. Organic edges that grow over time, adding habitat and raising protection as the sea rises.
Rain gardens and a climate-adaptive park. Low areas that hold water during heavy rain and cloudbursts, turning the most vulnerable ground into resilient landscape.

Connection

Softer waterfront. Organic edges replace the sharp, straight lines built for loading ships.
Streets for people. Permeable surfaces and pedestrian priority throughout.
Shared ground. Parks, gardens and multipurpose spaces for events and markets, including a market for produce from the farming pools.

Transformation

Grooved red facades. New housing keeps the site’s historic red colour, with grooved surfaces that channel stormwater.
Inward-sloping roofs. Roofs drain to interior gardens, cutting street runoff and watering green courtyards.
