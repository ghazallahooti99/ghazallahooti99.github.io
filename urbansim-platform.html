Method and sources

UrbanSim screens a district for urban heat and suggests where nature-based solutions (NBS) should go first and which fit each street. It is a screening tool for the early, strategic phase. It does not simulate microclimate or predict temperature reductions; it shows where detailed simulation is worth doing. The method follows my MSc thesis, A Context-Specific Nature-Based Framework for Heat Mitigation (Lahooti, Lund University, 2026).

What is computed

  1. Street segments. Streets are cut into segments of about 50 m.
  2. Sun exposure. For the chosen day, sample points on both pavements are tested every hour from 09:00 to 18:00 solar time. A point is sunlit when no building blocks the ray towards the sun at 1.5 m height. Trees and terrain are not included, so exposure is a worst case for streets that already have canopy.
  3. Destinations. Everyday destinations of older adults within 100 m: pharmacies, markets, bakeries, healthcare, teahouses, places of worship and tram stops, following the thesis categories.
  4. Surface heat. When a land surface temperature layer is loaded, each segment gets the mean of points within 45 m, ranked as a percentile within the area. LST describes surfaces, not what pedestrians feel.
  5. Priority. A weighted score of sun exposure, destinations and surface heat (weights are adjustable). Segments whose pavements are in sun less than 30% of the day are marked self-shaded and kept low.
  6. Street profile. Width is measured façade to façade where buildings line both sides, otherwise taken from the OSM width tag or estimated from the road class. Enclosure is H/W, classed as in the thesis: low < 0.5, moderate 0.5–1.4, high 1.5–2.0, very high > 2.0.
  7. Typology. 1 enclosed tram street, 2 wide open street, 3 enclosed car-shared street, 4 pedestrian street or square.
  8. NBS matching. Seven street-scale tools derived from the World Bank catalogue in the thesis are checked against each street with written rules. Every suggestion shows the rule that fired and the evidence behind the tool.

Evidence grades

  • measured field measurement or published evaluation
  • modelled published simulation study
  • guidance handbook or catalogue
  • assumed no evidence found; treat as a hypothesis

References

    Limits

    • No microclimate, wind or thermal comfort simulation. High-priority streets are flagged for UTCI or PET modelling before design.
    • OpenStreetMap heights and widths are often missing; estimated values are marked and should be replaced with municipal data.
    • Below-ground utilities are not known. Every in-ground tree needs a utilities check.
    • Destination categories come from an exploratory interview in the thesis, not from older residents themselves.
