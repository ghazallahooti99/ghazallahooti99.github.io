Weaving Landscapes

Interlacing the natural, the built and the cultural in Keldur, Reykjavík.

Typology Urban expansion as ecological and cultural restoration
Location Keldur, Reykjavík, Iceland
Tools Used QGIS, Rhino, Illustrator, InDesign
Timeline 2024 – 2025

The Question

Keldur, on the edge of Reykjavík, is set to grow. Growth here usually means sprawl, car dependency and rising CO₂ emissions.

The project asks a different question: how can urban design weave nature, built form and cultural memory into one living landscape, so that expansion becomes an act of ecological and cultural restoration?

Weaving Landscapes

The concept is a spatial strategy that interlaces three layers: natural systems, the built environment and cultural memory. It responds to Keldur’s existing ecology, its fragmented built edges and the Icelandic tradition of living close to nature.

Six Threads

1. A social anchor. A BRT hub and community centres at the heart of the district.
2. A green path. A walking route through the school and the senior centre, built for wellbeing, walkability and learning about ecology.
3. A green corridor. Planting along the highway that absorbs air and noise pollution.
4. An eco-park. A park by the Korpa River for biodiversity, recreation and everyday access to nature.
5. Growing together. Green roofs, greenhouses and community gardens that bring neighbours together.
6. Eco-villages. Clusters for circular living and local identity, blending into the hilltop neighbourhood.

Systems & Climate

The threads meet in a continuous green loop and a set of ecological corridors that restore native birch woodland, filter water and create sheltered microclimates.

Passive strategies, wind-sheltered spaces and seasonal flexibility let the district work through a harsh climate.

Culture & Phasing

Gardens, gathering places and vernacular forms reconnect people with the landscape and with each other. The result is a dense, mixed-use neighbourhood built in phases, beginning with ecological restoration and transit.

Weaving Landscapes is both method and metaphor: a way of binding urban life to its ecological and cultural ground, so that growth becomes a form of healing.
